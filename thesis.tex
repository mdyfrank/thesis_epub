% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% Unambiguous float identifiers.
% amsbook resets the figure and table counters in every chapter but prints them
% without a chapter prefix, which produces several "Figure 1"/"Table 1" entries
% in the lists of figures and tables.  Adding the chapter prefix makes every
% identifier unique (Figure 2.1, Table 5.2, ...) and, after \appendix, yields
% Figure A.1 / Table A.1.  Equation numbering is left untouched.
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\renewcommand{\thefigure}{\thechapter.\arabic{figure}}
\renewcommand{\thetable}{\thechapter.\arabic{table}}

% A small amount of emergency stretch lets TeX widen the interword spacing on
% the few lines it cannot otherwise break, instead of letting them run into the
% margin.  This changes spacing only, never the text itself.
\emergencystretch=30pt

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% Stable running heads.
% amsbook derives the running head from \sectionmark, i.e. from the *last* mark
% on the page.  A section that starts near the bottom of a page therefore
% overrides the head for text belonging to the previous section.  Suppressing
% the section marks leaves the chapter mark from \chaptermark, which is stable.
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\makeatletter
\let\uic@ps@headings\ps@headings
\renewcommand{\ps@headings}{%
  \uic@ps@headings
  \let\sectionmark\@gobble
  \let\subsectionmark\@gobble
  \let\subsubsectionmark\@gobble
}
\AtBeginDocument{\pagestyle{headings}}
\makeatother

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% Lists of figures and tables.
% amsbook reserves 1.5pc for the float identifier, which is too narrow once the
% chapter prefix is added and the number reaches two digits ("6.10").  The wider
% field below removes those overfull boxes, and the small \emergencystretch lets
% long captions break without running into the page number.
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\makeatletter
\renewcommand{\l@figure}{\@tocline{0}{3pt plus2pt}{0pt}{2.4pc}{}}
\let\l@table\l@figure
\let\uic@listoffigures\listoffigures
\renewcommand{\listoffigures}{\begingroup\emergencystretch=3em\uic@listoffigures\endgroup}
\let\uic@listoftables\listoftables
\renewcommand{\listoftables}{\begingroup\emergencystretch=3em\uic@listoftables\endgroup}
\makeatother

\chapter*{Acknowledgments}
My deepest thanks go to my advisor, Prof.~Philip S. Yu. Across my years at the University of
Illinois Chicago he gave me the freedom to choose my own problems and the honesty to tell me
when a direction was not working. Much of what I understand about finding a question worth
years of effort, and about recognizing when an answer is not yet ready, I learned from him.

I thank Prof.~Xinhua Zhang, Prof.~Sourav Medya, Dr.~Zhiwei Liu, and Dr.~Liangwei Yang for
serving on my committee. The hours they set aside for this work, and the questions they raised
about it, made its argument clearer than I could have made it alone.

My two internships at Amazon showed me what recommendation research looks like at industrial
scale. For that I thank my managers, Dr.~Edward W. Huang and Dr.~Yan Gao, my mentors,
Dr.~Jiangshu Du, Dr.~Nurendra Choudhary, and Dr.~Fan Yang, and the Amazon Scholars I worked
with there, Prof.~Danai Koutra and Prof.~Jia Liu. They took my half-formed ideas seriously and
were generous with their own.

I have been fortunate in the people I worked alongside. Dr.~Chen Wang, Dr.~Xiaolong Liu,
Weizhi Zhang, Yibo Wang, and Prof.~Hao Peng shared their thinking freely, pushed back on mine
when it needed pushing, and made the slow stretches of this work far better company.

Finally, I thank my parents, Hong Fu and Lin Yang, who trusted my decisions long before there
was any evidence that they would work out, and my grandmother, Zhen Tang, the very first
teacher in my life. She raised me from childhood, and the habits of study she settled in me
then are the ones I still work by.
